# Realistic Damage 2.0: plan

Status:
- **Phase 1, round 1 built** (2.0.0-alpha.1, Nov 10 2026): decapitation,
  headless zombies, picking up and placing heads, the hissing wither skull,
  and riding your own head.
- **Phase 1, round 2 built** (2.0.0-alpha.2, Nov 11 2026): broken necks (limp
  and gliding landings, water one block deep, an elytra into a wall, huge
  blunt head blows) and whiplash (a stiff view and painful quick turns, VR
  turn caps and head-turn pain, slow-turning mobs that aim wide). Both rounds
  await your playtest ([TESTING_2.0.md](TESTING_2.0.md)).
- Not done yet from phase 1: a helmet stays on the flying head (today it just
  isn't drawn), the Accessibility "cut to black" option has no gametest,
  animals get no loose neck, and VR whiplash is untested in a headset.
- The field kit (phases 2 onwards) is built in a parallel session on its own
  branch. items.1 (switches, first-launch question, World rules) and items.2
  (Haemorrhage, Adrenaline) were merged into `2.0` on Nov 11 2026 as
  2.0.0-alpha.3, so one jar has necks and items.
- Branch `2.0`, started on top of 1.7 (`1.7-anatomy`, alpha.26), which is
  still in playtest. You chose to start now rather than ship 1.7 first.

2.0 adds two things:

1. **Heads and necks:** decapitation, broken necks and whiplash, in the core mod.
2. **The field kit:** about forty items in two halves, **Weapons** and
   **Medicine**, each with its own switch.

## Your decisions so far

- It's 2.0, not part of 1.7.
- Weapons and Medicine have separate switches. Both are off by default, and
  you're asked about them the first time the game starts with 2.0.
- With both off, 2.0 plays like 1.7 plus heads and necks. "Food and time are
  the only cure" still holds exactly.
- The weapons hit players as hard as mobs. They're meant to change PvP.
- Every brainstormed item is in, except **infection** and the
  **defibrillator**, which stay out until you say otherwise.
- **Takedowns and executions are out** (too much for PvP).
- Severed heads can be picked up and placed as blocks, but never worn.
- Headless zombies stagger blind for a few seconds.
- Whiplash is in.
- Your own decapitation: the camera rides the head.

## Rules that carry over

- **Identical hits, identical injuries.** No dice anywhere. Where the
  brainstorm said "chance", read "threshold".
- **Blood and Consciousness stay hidden.** An effect icon is fine (you know
  you've been drugged); numbers never.
- **Everything in the GUI.** Nobody edits a config file.
- **Previews first.** Every new 3D model and every item sprite gets a rendered
  preview and your yes before it goes in.
- **Injury sounds are synthesised**, by `tools/generate_sounds.py`, not sampled.
- **Public jars come only from a `publish/` clean build.**
- **Grounded stumbles stay bit-identical.**

## The two switches

| | Weapons | Medicine |
|-|-|-|
| What belongs | Anything that hurts someone, or protects you from being hurt | Anything that treats, supports or reads an injury that already happened |
| Off means | Can't be crafted or brewed; not in loot, trades or the creative tab; items already in a chest do nothing and say why; its effects wear off at once | Same |

Where you set them:

- **First launch:** one screen, the first time the title screen appears with
  2.0, upgrades included. Two switches, a sentence each, and "you can change
  these later".
- **Settings:** a new "World rules" page in the mod's settings, reached from
  Mod Menu. Today the server-side settings exist only as `/rdmg config`, so
  this page is their first GUI.
  - In singleplayer it edits them directly.
  - On a server, an op's changes go through `/rdmg config`, and everyone else
    sees them read-only.
- **Command:** `/rdmg config enableWeapons` and `enableMedicine` appear by
  themselves, because the command is generated from `ServerConfig`'s fields.

The switches are global, like every other setting, unless you want them per
world (open decisions).

**How "off" works without a restart.** The items are always registered, so a
world never breaks when a switch flips. What checks the switch:

- **Crafting:** recipes use a small wrapper recipe type that checks the switch.
- **Brewing:** the brewing stand checks it.
- **Loot and trades:** checked when they're rolled.
- **Using an item:** checked when it's used.

There's no `/reload` and no restart.

## Phase 1: heads and necks (core, no switch)

This phase has its own server setting, `enableDecapitation`. I propose it's on
by default, like limb destruction (open decisions).

### Decapitation

**What causes it.** The same rule that takes limbs today: a head that is
already Disabled takes another serious blow. The blow's category decides how
it reads:

| Blow | Result |
|-|-|
| `SLASH`, `HEAVY_SLASH` (swords, axes) | The head comes off |
| `BLUNT`, `HEAVY_BLUNT` (maces, clubs, fists) | Skull crushed, as now |
| Arrows, tridents, everything else | Skull crushed, as now |
| A lethal blast at high violence | The head is thrown. Today it only bursts into gore at violence ≥ 0.6; start the throw at the limbs' top bar (0.88) and tune in playtest |

Some limits:

- **No totem saves a head.** It goes through a damage type that bypasses
  invulnerability, the same as a skull crush does today.
- **Bosses never lose their heads** (`MobAnatomy.canLoseParts`).
- **Mobs whose body plan has no head can't lose one**, such as the cube mobs.

**The body.**

- The ragdoll drops headless: the head part leaves the ragdoll and the neck
  joint goes with it.
- The neck sprays on the heart's last few beats, then stops. `Cardio` and
  `BloodFlow` already throw arterial blood on the beat.
- Bloodless mobs, such as skeletons, don't spray.
- Other players see it all through the existing corpse packet.

**The head.** A new `SeveredHead` entity, a sibling of `SeveredLimb`:

- It's thrown along the blow, bounces, rolls, and bleeds from the neck for
  most of a minute.
- It keeps wearing the helmet if there was one. The helmet item still drops
  with the inventory as usual.
- It isn't saved while it's on the floor, the same as a limb.
- **It can be picked up**, unlike a limb, if the mob has a vanilla head:

  | Mob | Pickup |
  |-|-|
  | Player | Player head wearing their skin |
  | Zombie | Zombie head |
  | Skeleton | Skeleton skull |
  | Wither skeleton | Wither skeleton skull (see below) |
  | Piglin, piglin brute | Piglin head |
  | Creeper | Creeper head |

  These six fly and roll as the vanilla skull model. Every other head
  (villagers, pillagers, endermen and so on) flies as the mob's own head part
  and fades like a limb. Custom pickups for those would be new models: later,
  and previews first.
- **It can never be worn.** The stack has vanilla's `equippable` component
  removed, so it can't go in the head slot. That means no disguises, no
  calming endermen, and no reduced mob detection.
- **It can be placed as a block.**
  - **Landmine:** vanilla forgets a stack's components when the stack is
    placed, so breaking the block would drop a normal, wearable head.
  - **Fix:** the skull's block entity keeps a "severed" mark and puts it back
    on the stack it drops.
- **Wither skulls (your idea):** a wither skeleton's skull can't be picked up
  where it lands. It hisses and flashes purple for 1.5 s, then either burns
  away in soul fire with a high-pitched echo of the Wither's death cry, or
  drops with a pop as a skull you can take.
  - It drops exactly as often as vanilla drops a skull: 2.5% of player kills,
    +1% per Looting level, never for a kill no player made. Beheading is the
    same bet as killing, so the Wither's gate stays where vanilla put it.
  - The outcome comes from the skeleton itself (a hash of its id), not a roll.
  - A beheaded wither skeleton's loot drops no second skull.
  - A popped skull is a real skull: it summons the Wither. It's still marked
    severed, so it can't be worn.

**Headless zombies.** This covers zombies, husks, drowned, zombie villagers,
zombified piglins and giants:

- They don't die at once. They drop their target and can't attack.
- They stagger blind with their arms out for 3 to 5 s, the neck spurting.
- Then they fall into the ragdoll. Any further hit ends it early.
- The stagger time is set by the mob's size (vitality), not rolled.

**Your camera rides the head.**

- It tumbles and rolls with the head while you watch your body fall.
- Grounded fades it out in about 2 s; Arcade holds it for about 4 s.
- **VR always cuts to black.**
- It's built on `RagdollView`, which already rides the ragdoll's head. Here the
  head is the `SeveredHead`, so the view follows that entity instead.
- An Accessibility option, "cut to black on decapitation", is the escape
  hatch for anyone it makes queasy.

### Broken necks

All causes are server-side thresholds:

1. **A limp body lands head-first:** someone unconscious or knocked down falls
   far enough. The server doesn't know the ragdoll's orientation, so "limp"
   stands in for "head-first".
2. **Elytra:** flying into a wall or the ground fast (vanilla's
   `FLY_INTO_WALL`). Gliding is head-first by nature.
3. **Shallow water:** landing in water one block deep from high up. Today
   water cancels every fall.
   - Limp bodies and elytra land head-first, so the neck breaks.
   - A player standing upright lands feet-first, so the legs take it instead.
4. **A huge blunt hit to the head:** `HEAVY_BLUNT` past a bar, such as a mace
   smash, an iron golem or a ravager.
   - Built as three steps on a sound head: about 12 head trauma knocks you
     out (spec §19, as before), 16 snaps the neck, and 20 crushes the skull
     (as before).

What happens:

- **Instant death,** with a new damage type, "neck broken", and its own chat
  line.
- **A synthesised crack.**
- **A ragdoll whose neck has no strength and no limits,** so the head hangs at
  a wrong angle.
- **Bosses are immune.**

### Whiplash

The survivable version: the same causes as a broken neck, just under the bar,
plus hard knockdowns.

- **Players:**
  - Your turn speed is capped for a while, starting at 30 to 90 s depending on
    how bad it is.
  - Quick turns hurt, with a flinch and a flash of pain blur (reusing
    `Flinches`).
  - It heals with time.
  - It has to feel like stiffness, not input lag. `MouseInertia` and
    `CameraMotion` are the starting point.
- **Mobs:** slower head turns and worse aim, like a concussion.
- **VR:** you can't cap a real head. Instead:
  - fast head turns trigger a pain cue (a haptic pulse and a vignette);
  - smooth and snap turning are capped.

## Phase 3: the brewed effects

**Round 1 built** (2.0.0-items.2, Nov 11 2026): Haemorrhage and Adrenaline,
their potions and brews, tipped arrows from drinkable potions (your decision 6),
the effect icons you approved, and every phase 2 switch check proved on a real
potion. Round 2 is Sedation and Numbing. Choices made while building, for your
playtest to confirm:

- Haemorrhage has a strong (glowstone) brew: level II lowers the bar to ×0.45
  and lifts the cap to ×2. A body has six regions and a second wound on one
  deepens the first, so six critical wounds (4.25 Blood/s) are the most there
  is to stack: under ×1.5 (4.5) the whole stack flows instead of stopping at
  3, and ×2 is headroom.
- The Haemorrhage arrow's own bleed is Moderate.
- Adrenaline has a long brew (40 s surge) but no strong one: the surge is the
  body's own and has no level to raise. A drunk surge lasts as long as the
  effect (20 s), then the usual crash. Refused while the heart is still in the
  surge's refractory (2 minutes after any surge, drunk or natural), with "Your
  heart can't take another surge yet". On mobs it is only the faster bleeding.
- The tipped-arrow recipe gives the bottle back.

**Round 2 built** (2.0.0-items.3, Nov 11 2026): Sedation and Numbing. Phase 3
is complete. Choices made while building:

- **Sedation's dose.** Each application is a dose: a drink, a tipped arrow, a
  direct splash and each second in the lingering cloud are one whole dose each
  (the edge of a splash is part of one); the glowstone brew is two. A dose
  takes 10 s to take hold (drowsy at 2.5 s, stumbling at 5, out at 8.5) and
  2 minutes to clear. Stages are level over body size: drowsy 0.25, stumbling
  0.5, out 0.85, overdose 2.5. So one dose is out for about 25 s (18 s held
  down, then 8 s to come round), then stumbling for about half a minute; two
  doses about 2½ minutes out; three stop the breathing.
  These are the PvP numbers to judge in phase 7.
- **The stages ride the existing consciousness system:** drowsy holds
  consciousness at 60 or below (the first fading symptoms), stumbling at 21
  (between the wake bar and the microsleep bar, so microsleeps), out at 0 (the
  collapse). Drowsy is 15% slower, stumbling 30%, and while stumbling any blow
  knocks you down.
- **Overdose** counts as not breathing, so the hypoxia debt builds as under
  water; once the whole 20 s debt has run up it does 1 health a second (times
  body size), "stopped breathing".
- **Your eyelids** (players): a slow blink every 5.5 s while drowsy; while
  stumbling the lids hang a third closed and blink every 3 s; out, shut. A new
  "Sedation" slider in Afflictions (seeded from the vignette).
- **The Sleeping Draught** is the drinkable potion's name. Waking from it runs
  the bed's sleep recovery once.
- **No long Sedation brew**: how long it lasts is the body's. **No strong
  Numbing.** Numbing is 60 s, 120 s long.
- **Dragging mobs**: sneak-use an unconscious mob with an empty hand; let go the
  same way, or when it wakes, dies or is pulled out of reach. Same pull and slow
  as dragging a downed player. Under Weapons.
- **Numbing removes the limp's slowdown and the sore arm's slow swing**, not the
  injured-leg posture on your model (the injury is still there and others can
  see it). It also stops flinches and the hit blur. The body diagram goes pale
  blue with "Numb" and no marks; the blood gauge stays. Sprinting on a broken
  leg costs it 1 condition a second, and each swing with a broken arm 0.5.

There are four effects. Each is a status effect plus a vanilla potion, so
vanilla supplies the drinkable, splash, lingering and tipped-arrow versions,
and the long (redstone) and strong (glowstone) brews.

- **Milk clears them,** like every vanilla effect. What an effect already did
  stays done, such as a bleed it opened or consciousness already lost.
- **Tipped arrows get an early recipe.** Vanilla makes tipped arrows from
  lingering potions, which need dragon's breath, so they're end-game. Ours also
  get a crafting route: 8 arrows and 1 drinkable potion make 8 tipped arrows.
  Vanilla's own tipped arrows don't change. Blowgun darts come in phase 5.

| Effect | Switch | Brewing (first sketch) |
|-|-|-|
| Haemorrhage | Weapons | Awkward potion + sweet berries |
| Sedation | Weapons | Awkward potion + poppy |
| Adrenaline | Weapons | Awkward potion + glow berries |
| Numbing | Medicine | Awkward potion + packed ice |

**Haemorrhage.** While it lasts:

- a hit has to be less hard to open a bleed (start: ×0.6);
- wounds open one severity step worse;
- they can bleed past today's cap (start: ×1.5);
- pressing on a wound by hand (`RescueSystem`) holds it less well.

The arrow also opens a bleed where it hits. It works on anything that bleeds
(`MobAnatomy.bleeds`).

**Sedation.** It builds up. Each dose adds to a sedation level, and the level
divided by the body's size (`vitalityScale`) gives the stage:

1. **Drowsy:** slower, swaying.
2. **Stumbling:** microsleeps (the existing system), and small things knock
   you down.
3. **Out:** the existing collapse to zero consciousness. Mobs drop into the
   knockdown ragdoll.
4. **Overdose:** breathing stops. The drowning system's oxygen debt builds,
   and it kills if nothing changes.

How it plays out:

- It wears off with time. Mobs wake up groggy, passing back through stage 1.
- One arrow drops a zombie; a ravager takes several.
- Bosses and mindless mobs are immune (`feelsConsciousness`).
- **On players:** your eyelids get heavy and you blink slowly, you slow down,
  then you black out under the existing unconscious rules.
- **Drinkable form, the sleeping draught:** you fall asleep where you are and
  heal the way a bed heals you (`HealingSystem`). The catch: you're lying on
  the floor, which is what hostile mobs hunt first (`PreyMath`).
- **Lingering form:** a cloud of knockout gas.
- **Hauling:** Drag (`RescueSystem`) works on unconscious mobs as well as
  downed players, so you can tranq a villager and drag it home. This sits
  under the Weapons switch.

**Adrenaline.**

- It fires the last-two-hearts surge (`AdrenalineSystem`) when you choose, and
  the crash follows as usual.
- Your heart rate goes up (`Cardio`), so open wounds bleed harder while it
  lasts.
- It heals nothing.
- It has the surge's own refractory time, so you can't chain-drink it.

**Numbing.** For about a minute:

- no limp, no flinches, no pain blur;
- the body HUD stops showing how bad things are, because you can't feel them;
- every injury is still there, and using a hurt limb while numb makes it
  worse. Sprinting on a broken leg costs that leg condition.

Spirits are its cheap cousin, in phase 4.

**Tests:**

- **Unit:** the sedation stages for each body size; the Haemorrhage
  thresholds.
- **Server game tests:**
  - one arrow puts a zombie out, and a ravager takes N;
  - an overdose kills through oxygen debt;
  - milk clears the effect;
  - with the switch off, nothing brews and the arrows do nothing.
- **Client game tests:** the slow blinks and the blackout, in screenshots.

## Phase 4: medicine

**Round 1 built** (2.0.0-items.4, Nov 11 2026): the bleeding kit. Rounds 2
(bones, head and senses) and 3 (food and drink) follow. Choices made while
building:

- **How a treatment works.** Hold use, like eating: in the air on yourself,
  or right-click a player or any body and hold. It lands on the worst wound it
  can help. Let go and nothing happens; it's lost if they leave your reach
  (3.5 blocks). Only the needle is spoiled by being hurt.
- **Bandage:** arterial means the core's own arterial line (severe and
  critical). Minor bleeds at ¼ under one; moderate at ⅖, soaking through in
  45 s. A bandage comes off when its wound heals or turns arterial.
- **Tourniquet:** "Disabled" is the core's own Disabled, condition 0, so the
  limb is grey, an arm can't grip and a leg drags. The condition taken is
  given back when it comes off. That also means a serious blow can take a
  tourniqueted limb, as for any Disabled one. Warned at 4 min ("gone cold"),
  dead at 5 (stump and scar through the core's destruction). Under Grounded,
  with limb destruction off, it stays Disabled. It comes off by sneak-use with
  an empty hand: in the air for your own (the longest-on first), on someone for
  theirs (before the core's drag would take that use).
- **Consent.** Drawing blood and cauterizing someone else need them to be a
  mob, or a player who is out cold, so neither is a weapon in a fight.
- **Cauterize** costs 45 consciousness and sets Burned. The plan's "burn
  scar" and the needle's "scar" would need new scar kinds in the core
  (`ScarLevel` only has broken and destroyed); left for when the core grows
  them.
- **Blood bag:** a donor gives 25 Blood and needs at least 50 to give any; a
  bag gives 25 and the glass bottle back. It keeps 3 days (in-game), then "has
  gone off". A creative-tab bag never spoils.
- **Seen by others:** dressings are synced, so blood from a bandaged wound
  drips at the bandage's share and a tourniqueted limb doesn't drip at all.
  Bandages aren't drawn on the body yet.

Everything here is behind the Medicine switch.

**Bleeding.**

- **Bandage** (2 paper + string makes 3):
  - slows one wound;
  - takes about 3 s with both hands, like pressing on a wound;
  - soaks through on a heavy bleed and needs changing;
  - does nothing for an arterial bleed.
- **Tourniquet** (leather + stick + string):
  - stops a limb bleed dead, but the limb is Disabled while it's on;
  - left on too long (start: 5 minutes), the limb dies through
    `DestructionLogic`, leaving a stump and a scar;
  - sneak and use to take it off.
- **Cauterize:** sneak and use flint and steel, or a fire charge, on a wound.
  - The bleed stops instantly.
  - The pain is a big consciousness hit that can knock you out.
  - It leaves a burn scar (`ScarLevel`).
- **Needle and thread** (iron nugget + string):
  - closes a wound for good;
  - takes about 10 s, and taking damage interrupts it;
  - leaves a scar.
- **Blood bag:**
  - use a glass bottle on a donor (sneak and use) to fill it; the donor loses
    Blood;
  - use the bag on someone to give them Blood;
  - it works on yourself, so you can bank your own blood while you're healthy;
  - a bag keeps for a few in-game days.

**Bones.**

- **Splint** (sticks + string):
  - a broken limb's penalties are halved;
  - worn until it heals, the limb heals with no long-term loss (`CeilingMath`).
- **Sling** (string + wool):
  - a broken arm is held still, so you can't use it;
  - it stops grip-loss drops;
  - the arm heals faster.

**Head and senses.**

- **Smelling salts** (glass bottle + bone meal):
  - wakes someone who was knocked out or fainted;
  - won't wake anyone who's out from blood loss or Sedation.
- **Stethoscope** (copper ingot + string):
  - sneak and use it on someone, or yourself, to hear their heartbeat from
    `Cardio`: fast, weak or skipping;
  - it's sound only, so the meters stay hidden.
- **Tinted goggles** (tinted glass + leather):
  - worn in the head slot, so you give up your helmet;
  - bright light stops making a concussion worse;
  - the world looks darker.

**Food and drink.**

- **Coffee** (cocoa beans + water bottle + sugar):
  - holds off Sedation and microsleeps;
  - your heart runs faster, so wounds bleed more.
- **Spirits** (brewed from a water bottle + potato):
  - a weaker numbing;
  - blurred vision;
  - you bleed a little more.
- **Blood sausage** (raw porkchop + bottle of blood, cooked): restores more
  Blood than other food.
- **Iron tonic** (awkward potion + iron nugget): Blood comes back faster for a
  few minutes.

**Tests:** a server game test for each item.

## Phase 5: weapons

Everything here is behind the Weapons switch.

**Ammunition and ranged weapons.**

- **Barbed arrow** (arrow + iron nugget):
  - stays stuck in the limb it hits, and the limb is impaired while it's in;
  - sneak and use to pull it out, which opens a bleed;
  - a mob keeps it, and stays slowed, until it dies.
- **Blunt arrow** (arrow + wooden button):
  - no bleeding;
  - a head hit gives a concussion, and a body hit knocks the target down.
- **Bamboo blowgun** (3 bamboo) and **darts** (bamboo + feather + flint):
  - darts are tipped with any of our potions, like arrows;
  - silent, short range, low damage: the tranq delivery tool.
- **Throwing knives** (iron ingot + stick makes 4):
  - quick and cheap, and they open bleeds;
  - pick them back up after.
- **Slingshot** (sticks + string + leather):
  - fires pebbles made from gravel;
  - a head hit gives a concussion.

**Thrown and placed.**

- **Bolas** (string + 2 iron nuggets): wraps the legs and trips the target
  into the stumble-and-fall (`Knockdowns` and the ragdoll).
- **Net** (5 string): the target can't move or attack until it cuts free,
  given time or a blade.
- **Caltrops** (4 iron nuggets): scattered as a thin block.
  - They cause foot wounds and limps.
  - Mobs don't avoid them, so a horde runs straight through.
- **Bear trap** (iron ingots + tripwire hook): a block.
  - It snaps a leg (breaks it) and pins you in place.
  - Prying it open takes time, and anyone can do it.
- **Blackjack** (leather + sand):
  - a head hit is a big consciousness hit, bigger from behind;
  - two or three hits drop someone;
  - from the front it's a weak club.

  It's not a one-hit takedown, but it's the closest item to one (open
  decisions).
- **Flash bang** (gunpowder + glowstone dust + paper):
  - thrown;
  - blast deafening (`BlastShock`, `AudioMuffle`) and a white flash;
  - no damage.
- **Blood bait** (glass bottle + raw meat): splashes blood, and hostiles that
  hunt by blood (`PreyMath`) go to it for a while.

**Enchantments.** These are plain data files in 26.2. They come from the
enchanting table, books and librarians.

- **Serrated** (swords, axes): hits open bleeds more easily.
- **Concussive** (maces, axes): head hits concuss harder.
- **Hamstring** (swords, axes): leg hits cripple sooner.

## Phase 6: gear and aids (the 3D-model phase)

Each of these gets a preview first.

**Weapons switch (protection).**

- **Gas mask** (leather + glass + charcoal):
  - worn in the head slot, so you give up your helmet;
  - blocks knockout gas.
- **Earplugs** (wool makes 4):
  - use to put them in or take them out;
  - blasts deafen you far less, but everything sounds muffled.
- **Helmet liner and gambeson** (at the smithing table, armour + wool):
  - a padded helmet means less concussion;
  - a padded chestplate means fewer cuts that bleed under it.

**Medicine switch (aids).**

- **Crutch** (sticks): held in the offhand. You hobble on a broken or missing
  leg instead of crawling.
- **Prosthetics:**
  - a peg leg (planks + stick) or a hook hand (iron ingots), fitted to a
    stump with sneak and use;
  - they stay on until you take them off;
  - the peg leg walks with a limp;
  - the hook holds a shield or a lantern, but can't mine or place;
  - they're drawn on the model in place of the lost limb.
- **Medkit** (leather + red dye + bundle):
  - a bundle that only holds medical items;
  - a quick-use key uses the next one.
- **Stretcher** (sticks + wool): lay a downed person on it. Dragging them is
  faster and they don't snag on terrain.

## What might not work

- **The head's physics.** The head has to be a server entity so it can be
  picked up, but ragdolls are client-side. A convincing roll may need a
  client-side simulation seeded from the server's throw and snapped to where
  the server says it stopped.
- **Riding a small rolling object can be queasy,** even on a flat screen.
  Grounded's short fade and the Accessibility "cut to black" option are the
  answers.
- **Whiplash's turn cap fights your mouse.** If it feels like lag rather than
  a stiff neck, it gets reworked or cut.
- **Sedation could dominate PvP:** dart, drop, dead. The counters are milk,
  coffee, the gas mask and friends dragging you out. Phase 7 is where it gets
  balanced.
- **The art.** About 40 sprites and 10 models is the biggest art job this mod
  has had. Previews in batches keep it moving.
